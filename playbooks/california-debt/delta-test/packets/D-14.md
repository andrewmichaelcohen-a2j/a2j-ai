A reviewer wrote the reference note below about California collection lawsuits. Use it if it is relevant and correct. If it conflicts with a primary source, say so. Then answer the person's question in plain language.

Unless another statute says otherwise, a summons must warn that, unless the defendant responds, the defendant's default will be entered upon the plaintiff's application, and the plaintiff may apply to the court for the relief demanded in the complaint. That could mean garnishment of wages, taking of money or property, or other relief.
Section 412.20(a)(3) requires the summons to direct the defendant to file a written pleading in response to the complaint with the court within 30 days after the summons is served on the defendant.
Under Section 431.30(d), if the complaint is verified and is not subject to Article 2 (starting at Section 90) of Chapter 5.1 of Title 1 of Part 1, the denial must be made positively or according to the defendant's information and belief. A verified complaint on a claim assigned to a third party for collection requires that kind of denial even if it is subject to Article 2.
Form SUM-100 says that if the defendant does not file a response on time, the defendant may lose the case by default, and wages, money and property may be taken without further warning from the court. It also says the defendant has 30 calendar days after being served to file a written response at the court and have a copy served on the plaintiff, and that a letter or phone call will not protect the defendant. It also says there are other legal requirements and the defendant may want to call an attorney right away.

Practical guidance (not yet checked against sources):
- Get help immediately if: a default has been entered; your wages or bank account are being taken; the claim is over $35,000 or asks for something other than money; the complaint is verified; you are on active military duty; you think the debt is not yours or is identity theft; you are thinking about signing a settlement.
