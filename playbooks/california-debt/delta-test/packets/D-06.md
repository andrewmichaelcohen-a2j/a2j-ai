A reviewer wrote the reference note below about California collection lawsuits. Use it if it is relevant and correct. If it conflicts with a primary source, say so. Then answer the person's question in plain language.

An answer to a complaint must contain two things: a general or specific denial of the material allegations (the key claims) in the complaint that the defendant disputes, and a statement of any new matter that is a defense.
Whether a general denial is enough depends on the complaint.
- If the complaint is not verified, or it is subject to Article 2 (starting at Section 90) of Chapter 5.1 of Title 1 of Part 1 (called "Article 2" below), a general denial is enough. It only puts in issue the material allegations (the key claims) of the complaint.
- If the complaint is verified and is not subject to Article 2, the denial must be made positively, or according to the defendant's information and belief.
- If the claim was assigned to a third party for collection and the complaint is verified, the denial must be made positively or according to the defendant's information and belief, even if the complaint is subject to Article 2.
Each defense must be stated separately, and each must say which cause of action it answers, so the defenses can be told apart.
Under Code of Civil Procedure Section 85, a case is a limited civil case only if every condition in that section is met. One of the conditions is that the amount in controversy is $35,000 or less.
Form SUM-100 says the defendant has 30 calendar days after service to file a written response at the court and have a copy served on the plaintiff. It also says that a defendant who cannot pay the filing fee can ask the court clerk for a fee waiver form.
Form PLD-050 (General Denial, revised January 1, 2024) says:
- A defendant who wants to file a general denial MUST use that form if the amount asked for in the complaint, or the value of the property involved, is $1,000 or less.
- The defendant MAY use it if the complaint is not verified, or if the complaint is verified and the case is a limited civil case (the amount in controversy is $35,000 or less).
- The defendant may NOT use it if the complaint involves a claim for more than $1,000 that was assigned to a third party for collection.
- The original must be filed with the court clerk, with proof that a copy was served on each plaintiff's attorney and on each plaintiff who has no attorney.
Form FW-001 (Request to Waive Court Fees, revised March 1, 2026) says that a person who gets public benefits, is a low-income person, or does not have enough income to pay for household basic needs and court fees may use it to ask the court to waive court fees.

Practical guidance (not yet checked against sources):
- Check the last page of the complaint to see whether it is signed under oath (verified).
- If the plaintiff is a debt buyer or the claim was assigned, get help before you file.
- If you cannot afford the filing fee, ask the court for a fee waiver.
