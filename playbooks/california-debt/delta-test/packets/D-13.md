# D-13: Letters, no court papers

Scope of this item: A debt collector's collection of a consumer debt, before or during a lawsuit. This item reports the 2024 federal text and the California cross-reference; the January 1, 2001 federal text that Section 1788.17 incorporates has not been saved. It does not address an oral dispute or a request made after the thirty-day period.

This describes the 2024 edition of the United States Code.
Under 15 U.S.C. 1692g(a), within five days after first contacting a consumer about collecting a debt, a debt collector must send the consumer a written notice, unless the information was in the first contact or the consumer has paid the debt. The notice must state:
- the amount of the debt;
- the name of the creditor the debt is owed to;
- that unless the consumer disputes the debt, or any part of it, within thirty days after receiving the notice, the collector will assume the debt is valid;
- that if the consumer notifies the collector in writing within the thirty days that the debt or any part of it is disputed, the collector will get verification of the debt or a copy of a judgment against the consumer and mail it to the consumer; and
- that if the consumer asks in writing within the thirty days, the collector will give the name and address of the original creditor, if different from the current creditor.
Under 1692g(b), if the consumer notifies the collector in writing within the thirty days that the debt or any part of it is disputed, or that the consumer requests the name and address of the original creditor, the collector must stop collecting the debt (or the disputed part) until it gets and mails the verification, the copy of a judgment, or the name and address.
Collection activities and communications that do not otherwise violate this subchapter may continue during the 30 days, unless the consumer has notified the collector in writing that the debt or any part of it is disputed, or that the consumer requests the name and address of the original creditor. During the 30 days, collection activities and communications may not overshadow, or be inconsistent with, the notice of the consumer's right to dispute the debt or ask for the original creditor's name and address.
Under 1692g(c), a court may not treat a consumer's failure to dispute the validity of a debt as an admission of liability.
Under 1692g(d), a formal pleading in a civil action is not treated as the first contact for purposes of subsection (a).
California Civil Code Section 1788.17 requires every debt collector collecting or trying to collect a consumer debt to comply with sections 1692b to 1692j of Title 15. It says that subsection (11) of Section 1692e and Section 1692g do not apply to the persons described in paragraphs (A) and (B) of subsection (6) of Section 1692a, or their principal. It also says its references to federal codes mean those codes as they read on January 1, 2001.

Practical guidance (not yet checked against sources):
- Keep copies of every letter, notice and envelope.
- If you want to dispute the debt, do it in writing and keep proof of when you sent it.
- If you want the name and address of the original creditor, ask for it in writing too, and keep proof of when you sent it.
