This describes Code of Civil Procedure Section 415.20 as it stands through December 31, 2026.
If the summons and complaint cannot, with reasonable diligence, be handed to the person to be served (the people described in Sections 416.60, 416.70, 416.80 and 416.90), they may be served in two steps.
Step 1: a copy is left at the person's home, usual place of abode, usual place of business, or usual mailing address (other than a United States Postal Service post office box). It is left with a competent member of the household, or with a person who appears to be in charge of the office, place of business or mailing address (other than a post office box). That person must be at least 18 years old and must be told what the papers are.
Step 2: a copy is mailed by first-class mail, postage prepaid, to the person at the place where the copy was left.
Service this way is complete on the 10th day after the mailing.
A summons must tell the defendant to file a written response with the court within 30 days after the summons is served. To count the time, leave out the first day and include the last, unless the last day is a holiday.
A replacement version of Section 415.20 takes effect January 1, 2027. Under it, a party shows reasonable diligence by trying in good faith to hand over the papers personally at least three times, on three different days and at three different times. In a lawsuit to collect a consumer debt, at least one try must be at the person's home or usual place of abode, if it is known or could be found with reasonable effort.
Under the new version, the mailing may be by United States Postal Service first-class mail, Priority Mail with tracking, or Certified Mail with return receipt requested. Service is still complete on the 10th day after the mailing.

Practical guidance (not yet checked against sources):

- Ask the court clerk or a lawyer which date starts your 30 days. Do not guess.
