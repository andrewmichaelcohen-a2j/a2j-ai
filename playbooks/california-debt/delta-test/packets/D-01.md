```
A reviewer wrote the reference note below about California collection lawsuits. Use it if it is relevant and correct. If it conflicts with a primary source, say so. Then answer the person's question in plain language.

Unless another statute says otherwise, a summons must be directed to the defendant, signed by the clerk, and issued under the seal of the court where the case is pending. It must show the name of the court and the names of the parties.
It must tell the defendant to file a written response with the court within 30 days after the summons is served on the defendant.
It must warn that, unless the defendant responds, the defendant's default will be entered upon the plaintiff's application, and the plaintiff may apply to the court for the relief demanded in the complaint. That could mean garnishment of wages, taking of money or property, or other relief.
It must contain this statement in bold type: "You may seek the advice of an attorney in any matter connected with the complaint or this summons. Such attorney should be consulted promptly so that your pleading may be filed or entered within the time required by this summons."
At the top of the summons, above everything else, it must carry this legend in bold type, in English and Spanish: "Notice!  You have been sued.  The court may decide against you without your being heard unless you respond within 30 days.  Read information below."
A county may by ordinance require the legend in every summons issued by its courts in any additional foreign language, if the legend in that language is set out the same way as the English and Spanish legend: at the top, above everything else, in bold type.
A summons in a form approved by the Judicial Council is treated as meeting these requirements. Judicial Council form SUM-100 is the summons form (revised July 1, 2009). It has the heading "NOTICE! You have been sued." and spaces for the case number, the name and address of the court, and the name, address and telephone number of the plaintiff's attorney, or of the plaintiff without an attorney.

Practical guidance (not yet checked against sources):
- Look for the court's name and the 30-day response notice on the first page of the papers.
- If you are not sure whether what you received is a summons, ask the court clerk or a legal aid office today.
```
