Under Code of Civil Procedure Section 337, an action on any contract, obligation or liability founded on a written instrument must be brought within four years, except as provided in Section 336a.
When that period has run, a person may not bring suit, or start an arbitration or other legal proceeding, to collect the debt. The period can be extended only under Section 360.
Under Section 360, an acknowledgment or promise is not enough evidence of a new or continuing contract to take a case out of the time-limit statutes unless it is in a writing signed by the party to be charged. A payment of principal or interest on a promissory note, made by the party to be charged, can start a new period. But no such payment by itself revives a cause of action that is already barred.
